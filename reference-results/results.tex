\section*{Abstract}
We compare a constant mean baseline, an ordinary least-squares line, and a
degree-9 polynomial on a one-dimensional synthetic regression dataset. The
polynomial attains the lowest training error but the highest held-out error,
a compact illustration of overfitting. All figures are single-split results and
not uncertainty estimates.

\section*{Method}
Data: $y = 2x + 1 + \epsilon$ with $x$ evenly spaced on $[-1,1]$, Gaussian noise
$\sigma = 0.3$, seed 42, 40 points. We fix a 20/20 train/held-out split with a
seed-42 permutation. Each model is fitted on the 20 training points only and
scored by mean squared error (MSE) on the 20 held-out points.

\section*{Discussion}
The polynomial fits the training rows best (0.0223 versus 0.0289) yet generalizes
worst (0.1429 versus 0.1035): extra flexibility bought a small training gain at the
cost of held-out accuracy. Because the evaluation uses a single split, these
numbers do not quantify uncertainty, and the held-out set should not be used for
model selection. Values were copied from \texttt{metrics.json} at four decimal
places and contain no new measurements.
